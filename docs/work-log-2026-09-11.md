# Work log — 11 September 2026

Internal. An SEO pass on the live site, and the ERP data work that fell out of
it. Figures measured from the deployed site, from the frozen snapshot, and from
live Unleashed on 2026-09-11 unless a line says otherwise. Eleven commits;
`5308815` (bulky freight consignments) is not mine and is not covered here.

**The through-line: four things that looked like our bug were somebody else's
data, and one thing that looked recoverable never existed.** Half the
catalogue's missing copy could not be fetched from anywhere and had to be
written. The sports bra size fault was in Unleashed, not in our parser. Two
"duplicate" machines turned out to be a plate-loaded and a selectorised version
of the same thing. And the ERP's own API will not accept most of its own records
back.

## What the site looked like at the start

Worth recording, because most of it was already right and the audit could have

live, self-referencing canonicals on every templated route, all 357 sitemap URLs

## Shipped

### 1. Product copy for half the catalogue

The largest SEO problem on the site. Of the 290 products in the sitemap, 145 had
a frozen-snapshot record carrying WooCommerce copy and **145 had none at all**,
so `unitDescription()` generated one sentence shape — "Buy EVA Exercise Mat (Set
of 10) at MASTERKRAFT. $2,000.00 inc. GST." — and that string was the meta
description, the JSON-LD description **and** the page body, on ~145 indexable
URLs.

**It could not be recovered, only written.** Checked three ways first:

| Source | Result |
|---|---|
| Frozen snapshot, 512 records | no slug match; 3 of 145 by fuzzy name |
| Live WooCommerce, all statuses | **524 products, none of them these** |
| SKU cross-match, 40-product sample | 0 exact, 1 prefix |

They are lines WooCommerce never carried — the apparel, the Concept2 ergs, and
machines added to the ERP after the store froze. The live store is still running
behind the old origin (`103.26.237.235`, reachable with a `Host:` header);
`WC_STORE_URL` points at the apex, which this app now serves, so it 404s.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Products written | **168** |
| Words | **13,640** |
| Max trigram overlap between any two of 14,365 pairs | **< 0.25** |

Written from the product name, group and size list, because the ERP's Notes and
AttributeSet are empty on all 1,425 products. **Nothing in it states a spec the
system cannot check** — no weights, dimensions, gauges, load ratings or warranty
terms. The test enforces that against an allowlist documenting the source of
every figure that does appear.

### 2. Entity decoding

Production served `<meta name="description" content="…Dual Pipe &#8211; the
versatile solution…">` and the same string inside the Product JSON-LD. Both call
sites stripped tags and stopped. **163 of the snapshot's 512 descriptions carry
at least one entity**, so this was most of the product copy the site has,
rendered wrong in exactly the two places only a search engine reads. Now 0.

The existing hand-written list of seven had already fallen behind the copy —
it missed `&#8243;` (the inch mark, in dimensions), `&#038;` and `&#8230;`. The
list's own mappings are unchanged and still run first, so no product or category
name was silently re-typeset.

### 3. Product schema

`brand` was hardcoded to `MasterKraft` on **every** product, including the
Concept2 ergs and third-party clearance stock. Now read from the ERP. Sampled
over 49 served products: 45 MasterKraft, 1 Concept2, 2 with no brand claim,
**0 wrong**.

Sampling caught one product claiming a manufacturer called **"Old"** — `OLD` and
`CLEARANCE` are ERP status markers, not brands, and title-casing them produced a
worse claim than the hardcoded one being replaced.

Also added `priceValidUntil` (rolling, so it cannot silently expire every offer
on a fixed date), `mpn`, and `url` + `itemCondition` on the `AggregateOffer`
branch, which had none of the three — so every ranged product was publishing a
thinner offer than a one-size one.

`shippingDetails` and `hasMerchantReturnPolicy` were **deliberately not added**.
`/returns` never names a return window and `/shipping` says freight is quoted by
weight, volume and destination — which is accurate, and is exactly why a
per-product shipping rate would not be. Recorded in
`handover-product-schema.md`.

### 4. Portal noindex

Five `/portal` pages duplicate the public content pages verbatim. `robots.txt`
disallowed them, which looked like it handled it and did not: a Disallow stops
Google **fetching** a page, so it never reads a noindex, and a blocked URL can
still be indexed from links alone. The Disallow is gone and a real noindex is
on a layout, so a sixth portal page cannot be added without one.

### 5. Listing fallback height

Lighthouse measured `/all-equipment` at **CLS 0.464** against a 0.1 threshold,
all of it attributed to `<footer>`: a spinner fallback ~400 px tall was replaced
by a 24-card grid several thousand pixels tall. `ProductGridSkeleton` already
existed, written in the initial commit and never imported.

| Measured on `/equipment/flooring` | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Page growth when real content lands | 4,279 px | **2,525 px** |

**The CLS score itself is not verified.** The browser pane here reports a 0×0
viewport and layout-shift scoring needs a real one — every reading came back 0,
including for the old code, which is meaningless rather than good. A Lighthouse

### 6. Listing payload

`unitCard()` builds every listing card through `unitAsProduct`, so attaching the
new copy unconditionally shipped 24 descriptions into every listing page that

## ERP data

Started as a tidy-up and became the day's most useful finding.

### Written and verified

| Code | Change | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `MAACU12M` | description `(S)` → `(M)` | **the medium became buyable** |
| `MAACU02-XL` | `Oversided` → `Oversized` | merged a stray record into its range |
| `MAACU02-S/M/L/XL` | `Obsolete = true` | retired the unpriced hoodie series |

`MAACU12M` is the one that mattered: its code said M, its description said `(S)`,
so the range rendered `["S","S","L","XL"]` — the medium unbuyable under its own
name, one of the two S entries unreachable in the picker. Now `["S","M","L","XL"]`,
with all nine of that record's wholesale tier prices verified byte-identical
after the write.

**Three hoodie URLs became one.** The spelling fix merged the stray XL record
into its range; retiring the unpriced series left one product. Both dead URLs
redirect straight to the survivor rather than chaining.

### The API will not take most of its own records back

Eight renames and three retirements are refused with:

> 400 `Product Sub Group is not a valid sub group of the selected Product Group.`

on records whose `ProductSubGroup.ParentGroupGuid` **is** their
`ProductGroup.Guid`, as served by Unleashed's own GET seconds earlier. The
discriminator is whether the subgroup **name** is unique in the 154-entry
ProductGroups list, and the correlation over ten records is exact:

| Subgroup | In the list | Result |
|---|---:|---|
| Unisex, Woman | 1 | **written** |
| Chest & Shoulder Machines | 2 | refused (4) |
| Speed & Agility | 2 | refused |
| Cable Machines, Dumbbells | 3 | refused (3) |
| Lower Body Machines | 2 | refused (all retirements) |

`POST /Products` resolves the subgroup by name rather than by the Guid on the
record. The only API-side workaround is renaming the duplicated subgroups to be
unique, which reshapes the category taxonomy the whole site reads off — a far
larger change than eight spellings, and not one to make as a side effect.

Getting that far also established that `PUT` answers 405, that `/Date(…)/` values
are refused in the format they are served in, and that unset sell price tiers are
refused as null. That last one is why every record was snapshotted first: three
of the ten carry real wholesale tier pricing — `MSCSPL09` runs ten tiers from
$1,343.21 to $2,513.64 — that quotes distributors and REVL franchisees.

### The duplicates were not all duplicates

`MSLBSE07` and `MSLBPL28` are both "Standing Hip Thrust" and are **not** the same
product. The Strength codes carry the loading mechanism at characters 5-6 —
`PL` plate-loaded across 42 codes, `SE` selectorised across 21. They are the
selectorised and plate-loaded versions of one machine, at $3,427.27 and $0.
Merging them would have deleted a real product.

**The site currently groups them into a single product page** at
`/product/standing-hip-thrust`, presenting a $0 plate-loaded machine and a
$3,427.27 selectorised one as two options of one product. Retiring `MSLBPL28`
fixes that on its own.

- **Four miscategorised machines.** `leg-extension-machine` and
  `seated-leg-curl` are filed under *Chest & Shoulder Machines*,
  `glute-and-hamstring-developer` under *Bicep & Tricep Machines*. Their codes
  say lower body. This affects category pages and breadcrumbs.

  description where there is room for ~155, no `ItemList` schema, and one
  sentence of body copy. `?page=2` also canonicalises to page 1, which is a
  known de-indexing pattern.
